AlbumSafe Privacy Policy

Last updated: 20 October 2026

Summary

AlbumSafe runs entirely on your phone. It copies the photos and videos you choose to your own Google Drive. There is no AlbumSafe server: your photos, your Google account details and your usage are never sent to the developer or to anyone else.

What AlbumSafe accesses

  • Photos and videos on your phone, including photo location data, so that backups are exact copies of the originals.
  • Your Google Drive, with your permission:
    • By default, only the files and folders AlbumSafe creates (Google's drive.file permission).
    • If you choose your own backup folder, such as a folder shared with your family, AlbumSafe asks for broader Drive access so it can find that folder and save into it. It only reads folder lists and the files it uploaded, and it never deletes or changes other files.
  • Notifications, to show backup progress and results.

What AlbumSafe stores

On your phone only: the list of albums you selected, your chosen Drive folder, the Google account email you connected, and a record of which files were confirmed on Drive (file IDs, sizes and checksums). This data is excluded from Android cloud backups and is removed when you uninstall the app.

Sharing

AlbumSafe does not share, sell or transfer any data. It contains no advertising, analytics or tracking. Data moves only between your phone and your own Google Drive, over Google's encrypted connection.

Google API data

AlbumSafe's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used only to provide AlbumSafe's backup features to you, and is not used for advertising or transferred to others.

Your control

  • Disconnect Google Drive in AlbumSafe's settings, or remove access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Your backups stay in your Google Drive, where you can view, move or delete them.
  • Uninstalling AlbumSafe removes everything it stored on your phone.
